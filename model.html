Simulator model and equations

How each block is calculated, and where the equations come from

1. Signal path

Each component is a separate module with its own ports, settings and equations. When you press Run, the components are run in the order of the wires (from the PRBS generator to the visualizers). The global settings set the bit rate, the sequence length (PRBS, 512 bits) and the samples per bit (16). The optical field is calculated sample by sample; filters and dispersion are applied in the frequency domain with the FFT. Optical wires carry the field; electrical wires carry the current (or the drive level); binary wires carry the bits.

The photodetector computes the clean current and leaves the receiver noise "pending"; the low-pass filter after it adds the shot and thermal noise for its own noise bandwidth Bn (Bessel 4th order: Bn is calculated from the filter shape).

6. Limits of the model

  • No fiber nonlinear effects (SPM, XPM, FWM), no PMD, no polarization effects.
  • LED and FP lasers are treated as incoherent sources: dispersion spreads their intensity (Gaussian spreading), not their field.
  • Q is calculated from 512 bits with a Gaussian-noise assumption; very small BER values are calculated from Q, not counted.
  • Qr, κ, the thermal noise and the filter bandwidth were chosen so that the results are close to typical textbook values (for example, PIN sensitivity about −19.5 dBm at 10 Gb/s).
  • The random noise uses a fixed seed, so the same settings always give the same numbers.

  5. L. A. Coldren, S. W. Corzine, Diode Lasers and Photonic Integrated Circuits, Wiley: laser modulation response.
  6. C. H. Henry, "Theory of the linewidth of semiconductor lasers," IEEE J. Quantum Electron., 1982: the α factor.
  7. T. L. Koch and R. A. Linke, "Effect of nonlinear gain reduction on semiconductor laser wavelength chirping," Appl. Phys. Lett., 1986: transient and adiabatic chirp.

  9. S. D. Personick, "Receiver design for digital fiber optic communication systems," Bell Syst. Tech. J., 1973: Q factor and BER.
  10. ITU-T Recommendation G.652: characteristics of standard single-mode fiber (dispersion formula and typical loss).
