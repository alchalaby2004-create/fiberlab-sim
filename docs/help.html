FiberLab Sim — Help

By: Dr. Ahmed A. Al-Chalaby · Version 1.20 beta

FiberLab Sim is a teaching program for designing and simulating optical fiber links. Components are placed on a drawing area and joined with links, as in commercial optical-system simulators. It runs in a web browser. This help explains how to use the program and, for every component, the governing equations, how each setting changes the result, and the sources of the equations.

1. How to use the program

  1. Choose an experiment from the list at the top: Free design, a Tutorial (ready layout, for learning) or an Assignment (build it yourself, personal values from your full name, graded).
  2. Add components. Drag a component from the library (left) onto the layout. On a phone, tap it.
  3. Connect. Drag from an output port (right side of a block) to an input port (left side). Ports and wires have colors: optical (red), electrical (blue), binary (grey, dashed). Only ports of the same kind can be connected. One output can feed several inputs (for example a fiber and a power meter).
  4. Set the components. Select a block to see its settings on the right. Press ? (Help) to see its equations.
  5. Press ▶ Run. The program runs the components in the order of the wires. Results appear above the visualizers and in the Results area.
  6. Open a visualizer (double-click, or Open graphs) to see its graphs: eye diagram, Q against sampling time, spectrum, signal in time.
  7. Sweep. Change one setting step by step and measure one result (for example Q against fiber length). Copy the table into Word or Excel.
  8. Global settings set the bit rate (up to 40 Gb/s), the sequence length (8 to 1024 bits) and the samples per bit (8 to 512) for the whole layout. Use many samples per bit only for short sequences (for example laser chirp studies).
  9. Export saves the layout as a file (.json); Import opens a layout file (you can also drop the file on the layout).
  10. Send result (assignments only, after Run): write your name and section; the program makes a short report with a verification code (your layout and results) and opens your instructor’s form, or lets you copy the report. The first time, paste the class code (starts with OSC1.) that your instructor gives you. Do not edit the code: the instructor’s grader calculates your layout again to check it.

To remove a block or a wire, select it and press Delete (or the Delete button). Reset lab returns to the starting layout of the experiment.

2. Signals on the wires

Wire Carries Unit
Binary the bit sequence and the time base (bit rate, samples per bit) bits
Electrical drive signal (0 to 1) or photocurrent A
Optical complex optical field, wavelength, spectral width, ASE noise level √W

The photodetector gives the clean current. Its shot and thermal noise are added by the low-pass filter after it, for the noise bandwidth of that filter. If no filter is used, the Eye & BER Meter adds the noise over the full simulation bandwidth (and shows a warning).

3. Reading the results

4. Limits of the model

  • No fiber nonlinear effects (SPM, XPM, FWM), no PMD and no polarization effects.
  • LED and Fabry–Pérot lasers are treated as incoherent sources: dispersion spreads their intensity.
  • The Rate-Equation Laser has no laser noise (no relative intensity noise, no phase noise).
  • Q is measured on 512 bits with a Gaussian-noise assumption; very small BER values are calculated from Q, not counted.
  • Some values (laser damping, adiabatic chirp, thermal noise, filter bandwidth) were chosen so that results are close to typical textbook values.
  • The noise uses a fixed seed: the same layout and settings always give the same numbers.

5. Transmitters

5.1 DFB Laser (CW)

Continuous-wave single-mode (DFB) laser. Narrow spectrum, constant power.

Ports: out: Optical (optical).

Governing equations

Output field

E(t)=PCW,ν=cλE(t)=\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{CW}}},\qquad \nu=\frac{c}{\lambda}

A single-mode (DFB) laser that stays on. Its linewidth is taken as zero (much smaller than the signal).

How the settings change the result

Setting Default Effect
Power (dBm) 3 dBm Sets the carrier power. After a Mach–Zehnder modulator the average power is about 3 dB lower (half of the time the light is switched off).
Wavelength (nm) 1550 nm Sets the fiber loss and dispersion (automatic values), the photodiode responsivity R ∝ λ, and the photon energy hν used for the amplifier noise.
Linewidth (MHz) 0 MHz Laser linewidth Δν (phase noise, a random walk of the phase). It widens the carrier line in the spectrum. 0 = ideal laser (used in the tutorials and assignments); a typical DFB laser has about 1–10 MHz.

5.2 Direct-Modulation Laser

DFB laser modulated by its current. Second-order response (fr) and chirp Δν = α/4π·d(lnP)/dt + κ(P − P̄).

Ports: in: Drive (electrical); out: Optical (optical).

Governing equations

Power levels

P1=P¯m+(1−m)/ER,P0=P1ERP_1=\frac{\overline{P}}{m+(1-m)/ER},\qquad P_0=\frac{P_1}{ER}

m = 0.5 for NRZ (half of the bits are 1).

Laser modulation response

H(f)=11−(f/fr)2+jf/frQrH(f)=\frac{1}{1-\left(f/f_r\right)^{2}+j\,\dfrac{f/f_r}{Q_r}}

Second-order response from the rate equations: resonance at fᵣ, damping Qᵣ.

Frequency chirp

Δν(t)=α4πdlnPdt+κ(P−P¯)\Delta\nu(t)=\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\,\frac{d\ln P}{dt}+\kappa\left(P-\overline{P}\right)

Transient chirp (α) at the edges, and adiabatic chirp (κ): 1s and 0s have different frequencies.

How the settings change the result

Setting Default Effect
Average power (dBm) 0 dBm Average launch power P̄.
Wavelength (nm) 1550 nm At 1550 nm (D ≈ 17 ps/(nm·km)) chirp turns into strong pulse spreading; at 1310 nm (D ≈ 0) chirp does little.
Extinction ratio (dB) 6 dB Low ER (typical 4–8 dB for a DML) → small eye opening. A higher ER means a lower P₀ and stronger chirp in real lasers.
Chirp factor α 5 α = 0 → no transient chirp. Larger α → wider spectrum → much more dispersion penalty after fiber at 1550 nm.
Adiabatic chirp κ (GHz/mW) 2 GHz/mW Adiabatic chirp: the 1s and 0s are at different frequencies, so after dispersion they arrive at different times.
Resonance frequency fr (GHz) 12 GHz Higher fᵣ → faster laser. If fᵣ is close to the bit rate, the eye shows ringing and closes.
Resonance damping Qr 1.6 Higher Qᵣ → less damping → more overshoot and ringing.

5.4 Optical Gaussian Pulse Source

Each 1 bit gives a Gaussian pulse E(t) = √P₀·exp[−(1 + jC)t²/(2T₀²)], T₀ = T_FWHM/1.665, centred in the bit.

Ports: in: Bits (binary); out: Optical (optical).

Governing equations

Chirped Gaussian pulse

E(0,t)=P0exp[−(1+jC)t22T02],TFWHM=2ln2T0≈1.665T0E(0,t)=\sqrt{P_0}\,\exp\!\left[-\frac{(1+jC)\,t^{2}}{2T_0^{2}}\right],\qquad T_{\mathrm{FWHM}}=2\sqrt{\ln 2}\,T_0\approx1.665\,T_0

One pulse in the middle of every 1 bit. C > 0: the frequency rises from the leading to the trailing edge.

Dispersion length

LD=T02|β2|,β2=−Dλ22πcL_D=\frac{T_0^{2}}{|\beta_2|},\qquad \beta_2=-\frac{D\lambda^{2}}{2\pi c}

For SMF at 1550 nm, D ≈ 16 ps/(nm·km) gives β₂ ≈ −20 ps²/km.

Pulse width after a length z

T(z)T0=(1+Cβ2zT02)2+(β2zT02)2\frac{T(z)}{T_0}=\sqrt{\left(1+\frac{C\beta_2 z}{T_0^{2}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\beta_2 z}{T_0^{2}}\right)^{2}}

C = 0: the width grows by √2 at z = L(D) and the peak power falls by √2. When C·β₂ < 0 the pulse first gets shorter.

How the settings change the result

Setting Default Effect
Peak power (dBm) 0 dBm Peak power of each pulse.
Wavelength (nm) 1550 nm Carrier wavelength (sets D of the fiber in auto mode).
Pulse width (FWHM) (× bit period) 0.5 × bit period FWHM as a part of the bit period. A shorter pulse has a wider spectrum, so it spreads faster (L(D) ∝ T₀²).
Initial chirp C 0 Initial linear chirp C. In normal SMF at 1550 nm (β₂ < 0) a positive C compresses the pulse first; a negative C makes it spread faster.

Use Bit Source › Pattern = Single 1 to look at one isolated pulse.

Sources: G. P. Agrawal, Fiber-Optic Communication Systems, Wiley.

Δλ≈1.45λ2kT\Delta\lambda\approx 1.45\,\lambda^{2}\,kT

5.6 External Modulator (MZ)

External (indirect) modulator, balanced drive at quadrature: E_out = E_in·cos[π/2·(½ − a(v − ½))], a set by the extinction ratio. No chirp.

Ports: in: Carrier (optical); in: Modulation (electrical); out: Optical (optical).

Governing equations

Output field

Eout(t)=Ein(t)cos[π2(12−a(v−12))]10−IL/20E_{\mathrm{out}}(t)=E_{\mathrm{in}}(t)\cos\!\left[\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-a\left(v-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\right]10^{-IL/20}

v is the drive (0 to 1). The modulator is biased at the half-power point, so the average output power is half the input whatever the ER. Balanced (push-pull) drive: no phase change, so no chirp.

Drive swing from the extinction ratio

a=1−4πarctan1ERa=1-\frac{4}{\pi}\arctan\frac{1}{\sqrt{ER}}

ER is the extinction ratio (linear). a = 1 gives a perfect 0 level; a smaller swing gives a brighter 0 and a dimmer 1.

Power penalty of a finite extinction ratio

δER=10log10ER+1ER−1dB\delta_{ER}=10\log_{10}\frac{ER+1}{ER-1}\ \ \mathrm{dB}

For ER = 20 dB the penalty is about 0.1 dB; for 6 dB it is about 2.2 dB.

How the settings change the result

Setting Default Effect
Extinction ratio (dB) 20 dB Higher ER → the 0 level is darker and the 1 level brighter (same average power) → larger eye opening and higher Q (smaller penalty δ).
Insertion loss (dB) 0 dB Insertion loss lowers the whole signal by IL dB.
Modulator bandwidth (GHz) 35 GHz The modulator bandwidth limits how fast the light can change. Low values round the pulses (ISI).

Sources: G. P. Agrawal, Fiber-Optic Communication Systems, Wiley. G. Keiser, Optical Fiber Communications, McGraw-Hill.

5.7 NRZ Line Coder

Non-return-to-zero pulses. The rise time is set by a Gaussian low-pass shape.

Ports: in: Bits (binary); out: Drive (electrical).

Governing equations

NRZ signal

v(t)=∑kakp(t−kT),ak∈{0,1}v(t)=\sum_{k} a_k\,p(t-kT),\qquad a_k\in\{0,1\}

p(t) is a rectangular pulse of width T (the whole bit).

Driver (rise-time) filter

H(f)=exp[−ln22(ffd)2],fd=bw⋅BH(f)=\exp\!\left[-\frac{\ln 2}{2}\left(\frac{f}{f_d}\right)^{2}\right],\qquad f_d=\mathrm{bw}\cdot B

How the settings change the result

Setting Default Effect

5.8 Bit Source (PRBS)

Makes a pseudo-random sequence of 0s and 1s at the global bit rate.

Ports: out: Bits (binary).

Governing equations

Generator polynomial (order 9)

x9+x5+1x^{9}+x^{5}+1

A shift register with feedback makes the bits. The pattern repeats after 2ⁿ − 1 bits.

Pattern length and bit period

LPRBS=2n−1,T=1BL_{\mathrm{PRBS}}=2^{n}-1,\qquad T=\frac{1}{B}

B is the bit rate (Global settings).

How the settings change the result

Setting Default Effect
PRBS order (2ⁿ−1) 9 A higher order gives a longer pattern with longer runs of 1s and 0s (up to n in a row). Long runs test the low-frequency response of the receiver. With 512 bits, order 9 (511 bits) fits once.
Pattern Pseudo-random (PRBS) Pseudo-random for normal links. Single 1 gives one isolated pulse (for pulse spreading or laser turn-on studies). Alternating gives 1010…, the fastest pattern.

The bit rate B is a layout parameter. A higher B makes each bit shorter (T = 1/B), so dispersion closes the eye sooner and the receiver needs a wider bandwidth (more noise).

Sources: ITU-T Recommendation O.150: Test patterns (PRBS) for digital transmission equipment. G. P. Agrawal, Fiber-Optic Communication Systems, Wiley.

5.9 Rate-Equation Laser

Current I(t) = I_bias + I_mod·v(t) drives the carrier (N) and photon (S) rate equations. Shows threshold, turn-on delay, relaxation oscillations and chirp.

Ports: in: Drive (electrical); out: Optical (optical).

Governing equations

Carrier rate equation

dNdt=I(t)qV−Nτn−vga(N−Ntr)1+εSS\frac{dN}{dt}=\frac{I(t)}{qV}-\frac{N}{\tau_n}-\frac{v_g a (N-N_{tr})}{1+\varepsilon S}\,S

N: carrier density, I = I(bias) + I(mod)·v(t).

Photon rate equation

dSdt=Γvga(N−Ntr)1+εSS−Sτp+ΓβNτn\frac{dS}{dt}=\Gamma\frac{v_g a (N-N_{tr})}{1+\varepsilon S}\,S-\frac{S}{\tau_p}+\Gamma\beta\frac{N}{\tau_n}

S: photon density. The last term is spontaneous emission that starts the lasing.

Phase (chirp)

dφdt=α2[Γvga(N−Ntr)−1τp]\frac{d\varphi}{dt}=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\Gamma v_g a (N-N_{tr})-\frac{1}{\tau_p}\right]

α turns every change of N into a change of frequency (transient and adiabatic chirp).

Threshold and output power

Ith=qVτn(Ntr+1Γvgaτp),P=ηhνV2ΓτpSI_{th}=\frac{qV}{\tau_n}\left(N_{tr}+\frac{1}{\Gamma v_g a\tau_p}\right),\qquad P=\frac{\eta\,h\nu\,V}{2\Gamma\tau_p}\,S

Fixed device values: V = 1.5×10⁻¹⁰ cm³, v(g) = 8.5×10⁹ cm/s, a = 2.5×10⁻¹⁶ cm², Nₜᵣ = 10¹⁸ cm⁻³, Γ = 0.4, β = 3×10⁻⁵. With the defaults Iₜₕ ≈ 33.5 mA.

Turn-on delay (bias below threshold)

td≈τnlnImodImod+Ibias−Itht_d\approx\tau_n\ln\frac{I_{mod}}{I_{mod}+I_{bias}-I_{th}}

Biasing at or above threshold removes the delay.

How the settings change the result

Setting Default Effect
Bias current (mA) 40 mA Bias current. Below Iₜₕ the laser is off between pulses: turn-on delay and a strong first spike. Above Iₜₕ the 0 level is not dark (lower extinction ratio) and adiabatic chirp appears.
Modulation peak current (mA) 20 mA Current added for a 1 bit. A larger value gives more power and more chirp.
Wavelength (nm) 1550 nm Emission wavelength.
Carrier lifetime τn (ns) 1 ns Carrier lifetime. A longer τn gives a longer turn-on delay (and a lower threshold).
Photon lifetime τp (ps) 3 ps Photon lifetime. It sets the threshold (shorter τp → higher Iₜₕ) and the speed of the relaxation oscillations.
Linewidth factor α 5 Linewidth enhancement factor. α = 0 removes the chirp.
Gain compression ε (×10⁻¹⁷ cm³) 1 ×10⁻¹⁷ cm³ Gain compression. It damps the relaxation oscillations and lowers the first spike.
Output efficiency η 0.4 Output (differential) efficiency. It scales the power: slope ≈ η·hν/(2q) ≈ 0.16 W/A for η = 0.4.

The equations are solved with a 4th-order Runge–Kutta method (step ≤ 0.25 ps). To see fast chirp details use a high Samples per bit (128 or more). There is no laser noise in this model.

Sources: G. P. Agrawal, Fiber-Optic Communication Systems, Wiley. L. A. Coldren, S. W. Corzine, Diode Lasers and Photonic Integrated Circuits, Wiley.

8. Receivers

8.1 Avalanche Photodiode (APD)

I = M·R·P. Shot noise grows as M²F: σ²shot = 2qM²F·RP·Bn. Thermal noise is unchanged, so a moderate M helps.

Ports: in: Optical (optical); out: Current (electrical).

Governing equations

Current with gain

I=MRPI=M R P

Shot noise with excess noise

σshot2=2qM2FRPBn\sigma_{\mathrm{shot}}^{2}=2q\,M^{2}F\,RP\,B_n

Thermal noise σₜₕ = iₙ√Bₙ is not multiplied.

Excess noise factor

F=kM+(1−k)(2−1M)F=kM+(1-k)\left(2-\frac{1}{M}\right)

k = ionization ratio (small k is better).

How the settings change the result

Setting Default Effect

FiberLab Sim — المساعدة

By: Dr. Ahmed A. Al-Chalaby · Version 1.20 beta

FiberLab Sim برنامج تعليمي لتصميم وصلات الألياف الضوئية ومحاكاتها، توضع فيه المكونات على لوحة رسم وتُربط بوصلات كما في برامج محاكاة الأنظمة الضوئية التجارية، ويعمل في متصفح الإنترنت. تشرح هذه المساعدة طريقة استخدام البرنامج، ولكل مكوّن: المعادلات الحاكمة، وكيف يغيّر كل إعداد النتيجة، ومصادر المعادلات.

1. طريقة استخدام البرنامج

  1. اختر التجربة من القائمة في الأعلى: Free design (تصميم حر)، أو Tutorial (درس بمخطط جاهز للتعلّم)، أو Assignment (واجب تبنيه بنفسك، بقيم شخصية من اسمك الكامل، ويُصحَّح).
  2. أضف المكونات: اسحب المكوّن من المكتبة (يساراً) إلى لوحة الرسم. في الهاتف المس المكوّن.
  3. التوصيل: اسحب من منفذ خرج (يمين الكتلة) إلى منفذ دخل (يسارها). للمنافذ والأسلاك ألوان: ضوئي (أحمر)، كهربائي (أزرق)، ثنائي (رمادي متقطع). لا يُوصل إلا منفذان من النوع نفسه. ويمكن أن يغذي خرج واحد عدة مداخل (مثلاً ليفاً ومقياس قدرة).
  4. اضبط المكونات: اختر الكتلة لترى إعداداتها يميناً. اضغط ? (المساعدة) لترى معادلاتها.
  5. اضغط ▶ Run: يشغّل البرنامج المكونات بترتيب الأسلاك. تظهر النتائج فوق أجهزة العرض وفي قسم Results.
  6. افتح جهاز العرض (نقر مزدوج، أو Open graphs) لترى رسومه: مخطط العين، و Q مقابل لحظة أخذ العينة، والطيف، والإشارة مع الزمن.
  7. Sweep: غيّر إعداداً واحداً خطوة بخطوة وقِس نتيجة واحدة (مثلاً Q مقابل طول الليف)، ثم انسخ الجدول إلى Word أو Excel.
  8. Global settings: معدل البتات (حتى 40 Gb/s) وطول التسلسل (من 8 إلى 1024 بت) وعدد العينات لكل بت (من 8 إلى 512)، للمخطط كله. استعمل عدداً كبيراً من العينات لكل بت مع التسلسلات القصيرة فقط (مثلاً لدراسة chirp الليزر).
  9. Export يحفظ المخطط في ملف (.json)، و Import يفتح ملف مخطط (ويمكنك أيضاً إفلات الملف على لوحة الرسم).
  10. Send result (للواجبات فقط، بعد Run): اكتب اسمك وشعبتك؛ يكوّن البرنامج تقريراً مختصراً مع رمز تحقق (مخططك ونتائجك) ويفتح نموذج مدرّسك، أو يتيح نسخ التقرير. في المرة الأولى الصق رمز الصف (يبدأ بـ OSC1.) الذي يعطيك إياه مدرّسك. لا تعدّل الرمز: صفحة التصحيح تعيد حساب مخططك للتحقق منه.

لحذف كتلة أو سلك اختره واضغط Delete (أو زر الحذف). وزر Reset lab يعيد مخطط التجربة الأصلي.

4. حدود النموذج

  • لا تأثيرات لاخطية في الليف (SPM و XPM و FWM)، ولا تشتت استقطابي PMD، ولا تأثيرات استقطاب.
  • يُعامَل الـ LED وليزر Fabry–Pérot كمصدرين غير مترابطين: التشتت يوسّع شدتهما.
  • ليزر معادلات المعدل (Rate-Equation Laser) بلا ضوضاء ليزر (لا ضوضاء شدة ولا ضوضاء طور).
  • يُقاس Q على 512 بت بافتراض ضوضاء غاوسية؛ وقيم BER الصغيرة جداً تُحسب من Q ولا تُعدّ.
  • بعض القيم (تخميد الليزر، الـ chirp الأديباتي، الضوضاء الحرارية، عرض المرشح) اختيرت لتقترب النتائج من القيم النموذجية في الكتب.
  • الضوضاء ببذرة ثابتة: المخطط والإعدادات نفسها تعطي دائماً الأرقام نفسها.
